How the predictions work

Three models, chosen and scored the same honest way: pick on June–September, then test on October–December data the models never saw.

1

You enter readings

Either recent usage (for a forecast) or the meter's reactive energy and power factor (for an estimate).

2

Models predict

Gradient Boosting and Random Forest models learned the plant's daily, weekly and seasonal rhythm from 2018.

3

You get context

Each answer comes with an error range, a comparison with typical usage, CO₂ and optional cost.

Reading the results

  • Range — about 80% of past forecast errors fell inside this band, so treat it as a realistic "could be" window, not a guarantee.
  • Typical — compared with the plant's own usage at the same time of day on weekdays or weekends.
  • CO₂ — uses 0.455 kg per kWh, the assumption in the analysis notebook.
  • Cost — only if you enter a price; real bills may also include demand and power-factor charges.

Good to know

  • The models learned from one plant in one year (2018). They are most reliable for similar operating patterns.
  • "Meter readings → kWh" is an estimate, not a forecast: power factor and reactive energy mathematically determine real energy, so it is very accurate. The page also shows the exact physics value as a cross-check when it applies.
  • The load-type label is the hardest part ({{ '%.0f'|format(100*cond.classifier_scores.accuracy) }}% accurate): Medium and Maximum often overlap.
  • Forecasts need your recent history. Use Fill typical values only for a rough what-if.
